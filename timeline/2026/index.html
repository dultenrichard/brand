2026 · Senior responsibility

Staff Cadet Sergeant · Blackdown CTC

Returned to Blackdown in 2026 as a Staff Cadet Sergeant, including two weeks as a platoon sergeant and four weeks in company float/support.

Staff changed the perspective. I was no longer there only to learn; other cadets, staff, schedules, equipment, and routine problems depended on people doing their part.

That experience sharpened my view that leadership is situational. To be useful, you have to adapt without losing the standard.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Two Best Advocate awards

Two Best Advocate recognitions earned through mock trial in April 2026.

Advocacy is not volume. It is understanding the facts, deciding what matters, building a case, and explaining it in a way another person can follow.

Those awards connect directly to my interest in political science, law, research, and public service.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Best Closing

A Best Closing recognition earned through mock trial in April 2026.

A closing forces you to reduce a large amount of information into the argument that actually decides the question.

That skill matters everywhere I want to go: briefings, research, leadership, policy, and professional communication.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Royal Canadian Legion Certificate of Appreciation

Presented by Branch 105 Cardinal on April 13, 2026, for service to its Ceremony of Induction.

This is community recognition rather than a competitive award. I value that distinction.

Service does not always need to be impressive. It needs to be useful and done properly.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Lord Strathcona Medal

Received at 661 Squadron’s Annual Ceremonial Review on June 2, 2026. It is one of the major national cadet recognitions and is tied to performance in cadet training, physical fitness, and commitment.

I do not see it as a finish line. It is evidence that the daily standard — training, preparation, fitness, and how I carried responsibility — was visible to other people.

It is one of the strongest formal markers in my cadet record, but the work behind it matters more than the medal itself.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Air Cadet Service Medal

Received in June 2026 in recognition of sustained cadet service.

This medal represents time: returning, staying involved, and taking on more responsibility as the years passed.

It only makes sense beside the earlier timeline entries. Service is cumulative.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Top Cadet Instructor

Received at 661 Squadron’s 2026 Annual Ceremonial Review.

Instruction is one of the leadership skills I care most about. A lesson is not successful because I spoke well; it is successful if the learner understood and can use it.

This award reinforces the idea that leadership has to adapt to the person in front of you.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Best Physical Fitness

Received at 661 Squadron’s 2026 Annual Ceremonial Review.

Fitness has been part of my cadet record through FSI, sport, and training. The award marks that work at the squadron level.

It matters because physical preparation is part of the standard I expect from myself, even when my exact future fitness targets change.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Royal Canadian Legion Poppy Appreciation

Community recognition associated with Royal Canadian Legion Branch 48, received June 2, 2026.

Poppy work connects cadet service with remembrance and the local community. It is not just another line under 'awards.'

I keep it because it represents service outside the internal cadet system.

2026 · Senior responsibility

Manufacturing SHSM completed

My Manufacturing Specialist High Skills Major is complete.

The SHSM combines academic credits with sector-focused certifications and practical learning. Completing it closes a multi-year school objective.

It shows a side of my education that is practical as well as academic.

2026 · Senior responsibility

205 community-involvement hours

My current recorded community-involvement total is 205 hours.

The number matters less than what sits behind it: cadet community service, poppy work, ceremonies, vendor assistance, dog walking, and other service.

I keep the total because it is measurable evidence of continued involvement, not because volunteer hours alone prove character.
